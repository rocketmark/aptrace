## Primary investigation: VVR-005 connection-induced activation

The most important failure is VVR-005 F-002.

With corrected Pilot Input Adapter polarity, Nathan demonstrated that connecting an inactive bench supply through the XLR cable could initiate one unintended movement. Once the connection settled, the AutoPilot reported approximately 3300 mV and remained quiet.

The failure was repeatable on first connection, despite no intentional trigger command.

Investigate which firmware-observable input histories could reproduce this behavior through natural original-firmware execution.

Consider transient LOW acquisition, input settling, analog filter history, sampling phase, startup or session eligibility, and the time between acquisition and motor initiation.

Follow the resulting execution through the recovered profile-playback, timer, and relevant STEP-generation paths.

I'd particularly like to establish whether a temporary input condition can produce one motor initiation after the input has returned to its normal inactive state.

Use bounded experiments and controls to determine which conditions are necessary for that behavior and which prevent it.

Do not assume Noxon's capacitor hypothesis is correct. It is one plausible electrical explanation, not an established MCU waveform.

## Secondary investigation: sustained false triggering

VVR-003 and VVR-004 document continuous unintended triggering when nominally inactive external sources were connected.

In VVR-004, the Rev 2 Spark+ output configuration produced an INFO reading of 0 mV, indistinguishable on the display from an intentional dry-contact closure.

VVR-005 then established that reversing Pilot Input Adapter polarity caused that same inactive connection to read approximately 3300 mV and remain quiet, while intentional dry-contact triggering continued to work.

Noxon attributes the difference to directional leakage through asymmetric external output circuitry.

Investigate what the original firmware does when its input remains continuously classified as active, including how program completion, eligibility, and re-arming affect additional motion initiation.

Determine whether the observed sustained false-trigger pattern requires repeated electrical transitions or whether the firmware can initiate successive movements from a continuously active condition.

Where natural completion or re-arming remains unresolved, pursue bounded analysis specifically to answer that question rather than expanding into a general motion-safety audit.

Keep external voltage, current direction, MCU observations, and firmware decisions distinct. The original firmware cannot establish the physical transfer characteristics of the V1/V2 input circuits.

## Tertiary investigation: VVR-002 periodic triggering

VVR-002 documents unintended movement approximately every 30 seconds following an early reset, gradually disappearing after approximately ten minutes and not recurring after a full power cycle.

Investigate whether any recovered firmware lifecycle could plausibly produce this behavior.

Consider motion completion, automatic re-arming, retained input history, Remote program execution, scheduled callbacks, and relevant reset or initialization state.

Do not assume the interval is firmware-generated. It may relate to the programmed movement duration, external equipment, or another uncontrolled condition.

If the existing evidence cannot reproduce the timing, identify what additional information would be required.
