1. Executive Summary
Additional validation of the AutoPilot Trigger Input was performed using a Mark Roberts Motion Control
(MRMC) robot in a commercial production environment.
During the validation session, AutoPilot initially failed to respond to several expected trigger sources,
including MRMC relay contacts, a 9V signal routed through a relay, and an independent 24V source.
Following reconnection of the system, these failures could not be reproduced, and both Relay Contact
mode and 12/24V Trigger mode subsequently demonstrated reliable operation through repeated
intentional trigger testing.
However, AutoPilot also exhibited unintended trigger events without any intentional input early in the
validation session. Although this behavior disappeared after approximately ten minutes of operation
and could not be reproduced following a complete power cycle, unexpected machine motion
represents a critical safety concern. Because the root cause remains unknown, the issue cannot be
considered resolved despite the inability to reproduce it.
The objective of this report is to document both the successful intentional-trigger validation results and
the remaining open engineering and safety concerns.

3. Findings
F-001 — Initial Trigger Failures Were Not Repeatable
Early in testing, AutoPilot did not respond to several expected trigger sources (MRMC dry contact; 9V
routed through a relay to dry contact; independent 24V). A Dragonframe DMC-32 positive control
confirmed the studio trigger plant could fire other equipment.
Following reconnection of the system, these failures could not be reproduced despite repeated testing.
Because the initial failure mechanism remains unknown, additional investigation is recommended. EMS:
AP-010 (closed as not reproduced; chronology retained).
Severity: Medium
Status: Open Investigation

F-002 — Critical Intermittent False Triggering
Early in the validation session, AutoPilot initiated stored moves without any intentional trigger input.
The unintended trigger events occurred approximately every thirty seconds following an early system
reset.
The behavior gradually ceased after approximately ten minutes of operation and could not be
reproduced following a complete power cycle.
Although the condition became non-repeatable, unexpected machine motion represents a critical
safety concern. The inability to reproduce the issue does not reduce its severity or eliminate the need for
root-cause analysis. AutoPilot simply cannot false-trigger in production use.
Engineering Impact
• Unexpected machine motion presents an unacceptable operating condition.
• The root cause has not yet been identified (firmware, hardware, electrical noise, initialization, or
other).
• The issue should remain classified as unresolved until corrective action has been verified through
additional validation.
EMS: AP-001
Severity: Critical
Status: OPEN — Critical Safety Issue
F-003 — Reliable Trigger Recognition Confirmed
Following reconnection and power cycling:
• Relay Contact mode operated reliably.
• 12/24V Trigger mode operated reliably.
• Approximately 20–27 consecutive intentional trigger events were successfully recognized.
• No missed trigger events were observed.
These results demonstrate that the Trigger Input is capable of reliable operation under the tested
intentional-trigger conditions.
Severity: Info
Status: Validation Passed
F-004 — 9V Signal Accepted in Both Trigger Modes
A direct 9V trigger signal successfully activated AutoPilot while configured for:
• Relay Contact Mode
• 12/24V Trigger Mode
This behavior was repeatable throughout testing.
Clarification is requested regarding whether this behavior is intentional or represents an unintended
characteristic of the Trigger Input circuitry.
Severity: Low
Status: Awaiting Design Clarification

F-005 — Diagnostic Display Limitation
During successful trigger events, the INFO page immediately transitions to the execution screen.
Because of this behavior, trigger voltage and diagnostic information cannot be observed at the
moment a successful trigger is accepted.
This limited the ability to capture trigger diagnostic information during validation and to distinguish
“signal not seen” from “signal seen but not executed” on future sessions.
EMS: AP-007 / AP-008
Severity: Low
Status: Enhancement Opportunity
4. Questions for Noxon
Q-001 Can the initial trigger failures be explained, given that they could not be reproduced after
reconnecting the system?
Q-002 What mechanism could explain the intermittent false-trigger behavior observed early in the
validation session (approximately every 30 seconds after reset, self-clearing after ~10 minutes, not
reproduced after power cycle)?
Q-003 Is operation from a direct 9V signal intentionally supported in both Relay Contact mode and
12/24V Trigger mode?
Q-004 Would it be possible for the INFO page to retain (latch) the most recent trigger diagnostics after
a successful trigger event for troubleshooting purposes?
5. Recommendations
R-001 The intermittent false-trigger condition (F-002 / AP-001) should remain an active critical safety
investigation until its root cause has been identified and corrective action has been verified. Do not treat
non-reproduction as closure.
R-002 Clarify the intended trigger-voltage thresholds and operating characteristics for each Trigger
Input mode (including F-004 / 9V cross-mode behavior).
R-003 Consider implementing a diagnostic mode that preserves trigger voltage and trigger-state
information immediately after a successful trigger event (F-005 / AP-007 / AP-008).
6. Remaining Validation
The following validation activities remain planned:
• Extended-duration stability testing
• Additional trigger-source compatibility testing (including known-good 24V path re-test after
recovery discipline)
• Long-duration idle monitoring
• Continued validation using MRMC production equipment
• Validation of any future firmware revisions intended to address the intermittent false-trigger
condition
• Capture of INFO idle / active readings where workflow allows
Engineering Assessment
This validation session produced two independent conclusions.
