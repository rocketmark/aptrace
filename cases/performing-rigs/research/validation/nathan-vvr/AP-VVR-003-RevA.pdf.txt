1. Executive Summary
Firmware package Autopilot_firm05-08 (Americas 915 MHz) was flashed and validated against the
AP-VVR-003 test matrix derived from AP-VVR-002 remaining work and Noxon’s latest trigger-mitigation
claims.
Positive results. Cold-start, soft-reset, and power-cycle idle watches (T-01–T-03) and extended soak
(T-13) passed with no spontaneous false triggers. Intentional Relay Contact (18/18), 12 V (12/12), and 24 V
(12/12) fires all succeeded with zero misses. INFO / monitoring remained usable during signaling
(standby and live mV readouts captured). Cross-mode discipline improved versus AP-VVR-002 F-004:
a dry-contact pulse while in 12/24 V mode produced no action; 9 V in 12/24 V mode also produced no
activation.
Critical concern. In Relay Contact mode, with a bench supply attached to the trigger input but not
commanding an intentional voltage (and again with a 12 V supply connected but not activated),
AutoPilot continuously false-triggered. Observed trigger-input readout during the fault was
approximately 1909–1958 mV, with occasional brief ~16 mV readings. Applying 9 V raised the readout to
approximately 2696–2719 mV. The same 0 V / 9 V conditions in 12/24 V mode did not false-trigger and
did not activate on 9 V.
Hardware scope limit. Only Revision 1 hardware was available. Batch-2 units use revised trigger circuitry.
Firmware behavior on Rev 1 is necessary evidence for field Rev 1 support, but is insufficient alone to close
engineering release for Rev 2 shipment.

3. Findings
F-001 — Continuous false trigger in Relay Contact mode with inactive / floating bench
supply
While configured for Relay Contact mode, with a bench power supply connected to the trigger input but
not sending an intentional commanded voltage (and similarly with a 12 V supply connected but not
activated), AutoPilot continuously initiated triggers. Resetting the unit did not clear the condition: it
re-activated immediately.
During the fault, the trigger-input readout remained near 1.91–1.96 V, overlapping the same band
observed for intentional dry-contact (~1.92–1.99 V). That suggests the input was sitting in the “contact
closed / active” voltage region without an intentional closure.
Aligned with Iker’s thresholds (2026-08-06): relay trip ≈ 1850 mV, clean contact ≈ 1950 mV. The fault
window sits above the trip — continuous fire is expected for that INFO band. Raising the relay trip slightly
is Noxon’s suggested mitigation, with long-cable margin caution.
The same electrical conditions in 12/24 V mode did not produce false triggers.
Engineering Impact
• Unexpected motion from Relay Contact mode under a connected-but-idle voltage source is a
critical production hazard if that wiring state can occur on set.
• May indicate insufficient discrimination between open / floating / leakage and true dry contact, or
unsafe behavior when a voltage source is paralleled onto the relay input.
• Requires Noxon root-cause analysis and corrective action before Relay Contact can be considered
validated on this firmware.

5. Recommendations
R-001
Treat F-001 as an open critical issue. Do not declare Relay Contact validation complete on firm05-08
until Noxon explains and, if needed, corrects the inactive-supply false-trigger behavior.
R-002
Do not grant unconditional engineering release for Hardware Revision 2 Batch-2 units based solely on
this Rev 1 session. Plan a short Rev 2 smoke of T-01–T-03, T-05–T-07, and the F-001 wiring case when a
Rev 2 unit is available (or require Noxon’s factory evidence).
R-003
Document operator guidance: until F-001 is resolved, avoid leaving a voltage supply connected to the
trigger input while AutoPilot is in Relay Contact mode.
R-004
Send this report (and voltage table) to Iker with emphasis on Q-001 / Q-002.
